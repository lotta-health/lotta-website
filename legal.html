Privacy Policy

Your privacy at Lotta

Lotta holds information about your health: what you take, how you feel, what you eat. That's not something anyone hands over lightly, so this document tells you plainly what we hold, why, who else ever handles it, and what you can ask us to do with it.

Every section starts with a short version, so you can get the answer in a line and read on only if you want to.

If you're in the United States, please also read section 17. If you've visited our website, joined the waitlist or applied to test Lotta, section 18 is the part that applies to you.

Last updated: October 2026 · Version 1.0

3. What Lotta knows about you

Short version

What you give us when you sign up and as you use the app, plus whether you have a subscription. Some of it is needed for Lotta to work. The rest is up to you.

Where it comes from

Everything Lotta holds comes from you: what you tell us when you sign up, and what you record as you use the app.

There are two exceptions. Apple sells you your subscription, so Apple tells us whether you have one and what kind. And if you sign in with Apple or Google, they pass us the sign-in details you agree to share.

We don't import your information from other apps. We don't buy data about you from anyone. We don't go looking for anything you haven't recorded yourself.

What you record, section by section

When you sign up

  • Your email address, or your Apple sign-in if you use one
  • Your first name and your date of birth
  • The country you live in
  • Your biological sex, your gender (you can choose not to say), and your height
  • Where you are in your treatment
  • What you'd like help with, and what's on your mind
  • When you'd like your reminders

Med

  • Your medication: which one, the dose, and when you take it
  • Symptoms, and how you're feeling

Eat

  • Meals and drinks, including photos of them if you want
  • Food allergies and intolerances, and foods that sit badly with you

Weight

  • Your weight when you start, and the entries you add after that
  • Your BMI, which the app shows from your weight and height

Anywhere in the app

  • Notes you type yourself

Behind the scenes

  • Whether you have a subscription or a free trial, and what kind
  • Your reminder and notification settings
  • Only if you switch it on: how you use the app (section 4)
  • Crash reports, if the app fails on your phone (section 5)

What's required and what's optional

  • Required: your sign-up details and everything in Med. They're what Lotta is, so without them there's no app to use.
  • Optional: Eat and Weight. Each one is separate, and you can switch either on or off at any time in Settings → Privacy.
  • Optional, and off unless you turn it on: app analytics.

If you leave Eat or Weight off, the parts of Lotta built on them have nothing to work with, so those features won't show you anything. Everything else works exactly as before. Section 9 covers what happens to what you've already logged when you switch something off.

How we look after it

Some of what you record is information about your health. We protect everything you record with specific security measures, which section 12 describes.

Notes you type are just notes

There are three places where you can type freely: a note on any log, a custom allergen name, and a food you tell us sits badly with you.

We store what you write, show it back to you, and include it in any copy of your data you ask us for.

The app never reads it. Nothing you type there is matched against anything or used to pick what you see. If you want something to change what Lotta shows you, record it through the app's own controls.

Your date of birth

We use your date of birth to confirm you're 18 or over.

7. How long we keep it

Short version

Your health record lives as long as your account does. Delete your account and it's gone, thirty days later. Stop using Lotta entirely and we'll eventually delete it for you, but we'll email you first.

While you're using Lotta

Everything you record stays until you remove it or delete your account. Your logs aren't deleted if your subscription ends.

If you delete your account

Everything goes. It becomes invisible immediately, it's recoverable for thirty days if you change your mind, and after that a job runs that deletes it permanently. Section 10 covers this properly.

If you stop using Lotta without deleting it

People sometimes stop using an app without closing their account. When that happens, we don't keep your information indefinitely.

So: if you haven't opened Lotta for twenty-four months, we'll email you to say the account is heading for deletion, tell you the date, and remind you that opening the app keeps it and that you can ask for a copy of your data first. If we hear nothing for three months after that, the account is deleted, with the same thirty-day window as any other deletion.

Altogether that's about twenty-seven months from your last visit before deletion starts, and about twenty-eight before it's final.

If you have a live subscription, none of this applies. We don't auto-delete an account someone is paying for.

The one thing we keep after everything else is gone

When we delete your data, we keep a record of the consents you gave, the ones you took back, and the deletion itself.

We keep it for two reasons. If you ever ask whether you agreed to something, the law puts the burden on us to prove it, and without a record we can't. And if you ask us to delete your data, the record is the only evidence that we actually did.

It's kept apart from everything else and it doesn't read as health information. Your identifier in it is a code, not your name. Your email address is held separately again, under lock, and the two are only ever put together if you ask us to or if a court requires it.

We keep it for six years after your account is deleted, which covers the usual time limits for legal claims in the countries we operate in. Then it's deleted.

9. Turning something off, and taking back your consent

Short version

Eat and Weight are yours to switch off whenever you like. When you switch one off, we'll ask whether you want to keep what you've already logged or erase it. Either answer is fine.

Turning off and deleting aren't the same thing

Turning something off stops Lotta using that part of your information from that moment on.

Deleting removes it. That's section 10.

You can do either at any time, from Settings → Privacy. They're two separate controls, and neither one is buried inside the other.

What if I want to stop tracking something?

Switch it off. Eat and Weight are yours to turn on and off whenever you like.

Medication and symptoms are different. They're what Lotta is, so they're part of the app rather than an option inside it. If you want to stop those, you're deleting your account, and section 10 covers what happens then.

What happens to what I already logged?

When you switch a domain off, we'll ask you one question straight away: keep what you've already logged, or erase it?

Both answers are fine. You might be pausing for a month and want your history waiting when you come back. You might want it gone today. We ask instead of guessing, and we write down what you told us, so if it ever comes up, we can show that you were asked and that we did what you said.

If you choose to erase, everything in that part of the app is gone within fifteen days.

If you choose to keep, it just sits there. Nothing reads it. Nothing uses it. It waits until you either switch the domain back on or ask us to delete it, either through this same route or by deleting your account.

There's no time limit on that, because you're the one who ends it. Choosing to keep your data is you telling us to go on holding health information for you, and we treat it as exactly that: your decision, live until you change it.

Does taking back consent undo what came before?

No, and we want to be straight about that. Turning something off stops Lotta using that information from that point forward. It doesn't reach backwards, and it doesn't make what happened before it improper. What it does is stop things, from the moment you say so.

Will turning something off change how Lotta treats me?

Only in the obvious way. Switch off Eat and the food parts of Lotta stop working, because there's nothing for them to work on. That's the switch doing what it says.

Everything else carries on exactly as before. We won't degrade the rest of the app, and we won't treat you differently for having used a right that's yours.

10. Deleting your account

Short version

Everything disappears straight away, you've got thirty days to change your mind, and then it's permanently gone. Deleting your account does not cancel your Apple subscription. Only you can do that.

What happens when you tap delete

Everything becomes invisible immediately. You can still sign in, but there's nothing there.

For thirty days, you can cancel the deletion and get everything back exactly as it was.

After thirty days, a job runs and removes it permanently. That job deletes; it can't read.

The only thing that survives is the record described at the end of section 7: that you consented, that you withdrew, that you deleted. Nothing about your health.

Your subscription is a separate thing, and this part matters

Deleting your Lotta account does not cancel your Apple subscription. We can't cancel it for you. Nobody at Lotta can. An App Store subscription belongs to your Apple ID, and only you can end it, from your Apple settings.

If you delete your account without cancelling, you'll keep being charged for an app showing you nothing. So do that first. The deletion screen points you to the right place in your Apple settings.

If you're on a free trial, the same applies. Deleting your account doesn't cancel it, and unless you cancel it through Apple before it ends, it turns into a paid subscription.

Once you cancel, your subscription runs to the end of the period you've already paid for. Anything to do with refunds is handled by Apple, under Apple's terms, and you can ask them directly.

Cancelling your subscription is not the same as deleting your account

If you cancel or let a subscription lapse, nothing is deleted. Your account stays, your logs stay, and you can ask us for a copy of your data at any time.

If you've asked us for something and then delete

If you've asked for a copy of your data, or made any other request, we finish answering it before the deletion completes. We won't destroy the record we need in order to answer you.

11. When a person can see your data

Short version

By default, nobody at Lotta can look at your health record. There are a few narrow exceptions, they're listed here, and every single access is logged.

The default

Lotta is built so that we can't casually look. There's no admin screen anywhere that displays your health information, so there's nothing for one of us to idly open.

When we can look, and only then

When you've reported a problem and asked us to. You tell us something's wrong; we ask your permission in the app; you say yes; we look at the part you reported, for as long as it takes to fix it. Not your whole history.

When you've made a request under section 8 and we need to reach your data to answer it.

When a court or the law requires it. Only what's specified, nothing more.

When we're investigating fraud or abuse of the app. Your account and how it's been used, not your health record.

Billing questions never need health information, and don't get it.

Every access is written down

Each time someone reaches your data, it's recorded: who, when, and why. That record exists so the rules above can be checked rather than just claimed.

Anyone who could ever reach your health information, including anyone we work with, is under a written duty of confidentiality before they get anywhere near it.

18. Our website, the waitlist and the beta

Short version

If you've visited our website, joined the waitlist or applied to test Lotta, this is the part that applies to you. Once you use the app itself, the rest of this policy applies too.

Visiting our website

Our legal reason is our legitimate interest in knowing how the site is used, and you can object (section 8).

Cookies

Our website doesn't use cookies, and it doesn't save anything else on your device.

We count visits with PostHog, in a mode that saves nothing on your device. When you open a page or press a button, PostHog receives the page's address, the page you came from, any campaign label in the link you followed, your type of browser and device, and your screen size. To tell visits apart within a day, PostHog turns your internet address and browser into a scrambled code using a key that changes every day, then removes your internet address. A visit on one day can't be linked to a visit on another, and we never learn who you are. We don't use any of this for advertising.

Like any website, our host (GitHub) and the services that deliver our fonts and code (Google Fonts, Cloudflare and jsDelivr) receive your internet address when your browser asks them for those files.

Your choices: you can object to the visit counting (section 8), or block it with your browser's privacy settings or a content blocker. The site works the same either way.
